### Project Description
[AI il Thaalikett is a proudly useless web app built for TinkerHub’s Useless Projects hackathon that simulates a South Indian wedding right inside your browser using HTML5 Canvas and vanilla JavaScript. You upload photos of the couple, pick a wedding vibe, and hit a button to watch the groom's arm magically stretch across the screen to tie the Thaali with full audio and confetti effects.]

### The Problem (that doesn't exist)
[Because humanity was desperately waiting for a way to ruin marriages without the hassle of catering bills, overpriced venues, or actual human interaction. Why spend months planning a traditional ceremony when you can just upload two JPEG faces into a web browser, press a button, and watch a digital groom's arm stretch out like a cartoon character to tie a virtual knot? It completely solves the terrifying crisis of distant relatives actually missing the exact second of a wedding on a blurry livestream by replacing real memories with glitchy canvas animations, looping audio tracks of church organs and nadaswarams, and a confetti cannon that screams "we have too much free time". Truly a groundbreaking leap forward for civilization that nobody asked for.]

### The Solution (that nobody asked for)
[We boldly tackled this non-existent crisis by inventing a fully automated, entirely digital matrimony engine that strips away all the romance, legal validity, and common sense from a wedding. Instead of booking a convention center, users can now map their faces onto pixel-art avatars and watch a terrifyingly stretchy groom's arm perform an infinite loop of the Thaalikett ceremony right inside an HTML5 canvas. To make the dystopian experience complete, we slapped together an unhinged Web Audio soundboard so you can violently blast church organs, nadaswarams, and gossip tracks while digital confetti cannons fire away—proving once and for all that technology was a mistake and nobody should ever be trusted with an afternoon of free time.]
